How to think about the Developer and Verifier personas in HUMAIN ONE.

Version 1 · 24 September 2026 · Discussion paper · Approximately 10 minutes

Recommended product model

The developer improves the agent. The verifier independently decides whether a specific version meets the requirements for a specific destination.

Both can run Eval. What changes is who owns the test, who controls the environment, and who can make the admission decision.

The product thesis

Use the existing Eval foundation for two review authorities: tenant acceptance and HUMAIN marketplace admission. Give each its own scope and decision record, connected to the same submitted agent version.

01

Three jobs, with different decision rights

Developer and Verifier are roles. Tenant and HUMAIN describe the scope of the verifier's authority. A role does not need a separate product or portal.

Developer

“What should I change to make this agent ready?”

Owns
Agent behavior, packaging, declared permissions, functional evals and remediation.
Needs
Fast feedback, visible submission requirements, reproducible failures and a clear route back from review.
Produces
A versioned package, evidence packet, intended use and known limitations.
Boundary
Can submit and respond. Cannot grant marketplace approval to their own submission.
Tenant verifier

“Does this version work for our organization?”

Owns
Tenant acceptance review, including SDLC QA and independent functional evaluation.
Needs
Its own datasets, metrics and test access; comparable evidence; actionable findings.
Produces
An acceptance decision scoped to the tenant workspace and tested use case.
Boundary
Tenant approval does not confer marketplace approval.
HUMAIN verifier

“Can the marketplace admit this version?”

Owns
Application of HUMAIN's marketplace requirements and review of security and policy evidence.
Needs
A common baseline, independent execution, Guardian findings and a defined decision policy.
Produces
A marketplace admission decision with evidence, scope and unresolved limitations.
Boundary
Marketplace acceptance does not prove fitness for every tenant's workflow.
Turing and Aramco are examples, not permanent role categories. An organization can supply developers and tenant verifiers; HUMAIN marketplace authority is a separate scope. Keep platform administration separate. Provisioning environments, managing role grants and operating shared infrastructure are administrative responsibilities. The verifier specifies the review needs; having a verifier role should not automatically grant broad tenant-admin access. This is a recommended permission model.
02

The handoff transfers accountability for a decision

The developer continues to own the agent. Submission creates a stable object that someone else can inspect, test and decide on.

→
Submit for reviewChoose the intended destination and declare limitations
→
A fixed submissionAgent version + manifest + evidence references + review scope

Tenant review → workspace

Independent QA, tenant-specific evidence and an acceptance decision.

HUMAIN review → marketplace

Common baseline, Guardian and policy evidence, then an admission decision.

Findings → developer fixes → new agent version → linked review cycle

Conceptual routes, not a confirmed execution order. Whether HUMAIN review requires prior tenant acceptance remains open. Approval and actual publication are separate states.

Recommendation

Make evidence reusable; keep judgments scoped

A review record should bind the artifact version, evaluation setup, environment, requirements and reviewer decision. Reuse an existing run only when those inputs are applicable and its provenance is sufficient.

Different datasets or environments may produce different results. Show the differences before presenting a comparison as like-for-like.

Evolution from the August viewpoint

The boundary cannot be “who runs Eval”

The earlier model placed the reviewer after developer evaluation. Sandip's newer notes explicitly include verifier-owned reruns. Preserve developer iteration while adding independent evaluation inside review.

The older internal-first sequence, conditional approvals and verifier-owned publication are historical assumptions to reconfirm, not settled requirements for this design.

04

Walk the same agent through three situations

Illustrative procurement assistant. Select a situation to see how the developer and verifier relationship changes. All events and findings below are examples.

Illustrative workflow

Good developer results; a tenant-specific failure

Developer submits v1.4

The agent answers procurement questions well on the developer's examples. Its package and Eval evidence are ready for tenant review.

Tenant verifier reruns

With the tenant's own policy documents and Arabic cases, the agent applies an outdated approval limit. The reviewer records the failed case and relevant evidence.

Developer fixes the behavior

The developer updates retrieval/configuration and submits v1.5. The reviewer retests the finding and the required coverage before deciding on workspace acceptance.

The verifier creates independent evidence. A developer's good score alone cannot establish fitness for the tenant's policy context.
Illustrative workflow

Tenant acceptance does not settle marketplace admission

Developer targets marketplace

The package declares tool access, external connections, intended use and known gaps. Tenant evidence may be included, but its acceptance is not treated as a marketplace decision.

HUMAIN reviews independently

A proposed adversarial test finds that a malicious supplier document can induce an unauthorized outbound request. Evidence is attached to a finding. This is not a claim about current Guardian coverage.

Review returns a scoped result

Under an agreed blocking rule, the reviewer requests a fix. Once required evidence is complete, the authorized party can approve that version for the marketplace; publication follows the agreed policy.

A shared baseline should apply consistently across submitting tenants. Additional use-case or jurisdiction requirements need an explicit applicability policy.
Illustrative workflow

A small code change can expand the permission boundary

v1.6 adds an action

The agent can now send purchase orders, whereas v1.5 only drafted them. The developer submits a new package and permission declaration.

Verifier sees what changed

The review highlights the new tool permission, affected tests and previous findings. Whether unaffected evidence may be reused follows an agreed change policy.

A new decision is required

Approval for v1.5 does not silently apply to v1.6. The decision record names the new version and destination. Existing publication status remains separate.

Version the reviewed context: artifact, configuration, tools, model references and policy. Material changes can invalidate prior evidence even when the agent's display name is unchanged.

06

Verification needs several kinds of evidence

A quality metric, a security finding and a policy assessment should retain their individual meaning. One blended score can hide a blocking issue.

A
Submission readiness

Can the package be identified and run? Check manifest completeness, artifact identity, test access and evidence references. Passing this layer admits a submission to review.

B
Task quality

Does the agent meet the intended use? Tenant examples, domain metrics, language coverage and failure inspection belong here. Reuse Eval and label who produced each run.

C
Agent and application security

Candidate tests: malicious retrieved instructions, tool misuse, cross-tenant access, data exfiltration and uncontrolled action loops. Scope tests to the agent's actual capabilities.

D
Policy and compliance evidence

Map an applicable requirement to its evidence, assessment and owner. Record not-tested and not-applicable distinctly. A framework name alone does not establish compliance.

Framework references

Use each reference for the job it does

NIST AI RMF: a structure for risk governance and context. Select the actual document/version and map relevant outcomes to the review policy. Source.

OWASP Agentic Top 10: a starting point for agent-specific threat coverage. The proposed tests on this page are design examples, not a claim of complete OWASP coverage. Source.

Saudi requirements: identify the precise SDAIA data/privacy and NCA cybersecurity references. “TDPL” in the discussion is unresolved; confirm whether PDPL was intended. SDAIA · NCA data controls.

Open: consumer country is one proposed input. Applicability also needs intended use, data and deployment context, with the mapping confirmed by the responsible policy owner.

A sandbox must make the review independent

Recommend verifier-controlled execution of the submitted artifact, scoped test credentials, controlled network access, protected review data and retained evidence. Reuse platform provisioning where it satisfies that boundary.

A separate screen or namespace does not, by itself, prove isolation. Architecture, ownership, cost and coverage for each verifier type remain open. If provisioning fails, show “Blocked”; do not turn it into a failed agent test.

Guardian supplies evidence to the decision

The project-supplied integration guide describes findings and qualitative recommendations; the consuming platform owns the gate decision. Confirm that this remains the current service contract.

Model audit status, evidence and reviewer judgment separately. An unavailable audit is not a pass; a completed audit may still contain unresolved findings or coverage limits. The internal guide is summarized here, not republished.

07

Build the review journey on the Eval foundation

Recommended next increment: one complete submission, independent review, returned finding, corrected version and scoped decision. Demonstrate both destinations.

Reuse

The objects already understood

Datasets, metrics, experiment templates, evaluation runs, case results, traces and the developer evidence packet.

Keep developer iteration available before submission and during remediation. In the existing Eval journey, the evidence packet is the entry point for requesting verification.

Add

The review context around them

A submission queue, reviewer assignment, scoped test setup, independent findings, a decision record and review history.

Place them in the existing HUMAIN ONE experience behind appropriate permissions. Validate navigation with reviewers before deciding whether a dedicated workspace is needed.

1
Review queue

Who needs to act? Show authority, target destination, version, current owner, age and blockers. Keep tenant and marketplace queues scoped to the viewer.

2
Submission and evidence

What exactly am I reviewing? Show package identity, developer claims, run provenance, declared permissions, known gaps and previous review history.

3
Independent test setup

What can I verify? Choose permitted datasets, metrics and environment. Display readiness, access failures and required checks without reproducing infrastructure administration.

4
Findings and corrections

What needs to change? Each finding names the failing requirement, evidence, affected version and retest route. Developer feedback should be actionable while respecting protected test data.

5
Decision and publication status

Who approved what? Record authority, version, destination, policy version, rationale and time. Keep Approved, Published, Changes requested and Rejected distinguishable.

First-demo acceptance bar: evidence from the wrong version cannot satisfy a gate; missing required tests cannot appear passed; blocked execution is visible; corrections preserve history; one authority's approval cannot unlock the other's destination.

Later, once policy is defined: bulk triage, formal exceptions, review expiry and runtime-triggered re-verification. Prototype states and integrations must remain clearly labeled until implemented.

08

Four agreements unlock the prototype

Take a proposed contract into the next discussion, with concrete choices for HUMAIN to resolve.

01 / Authority

Who decides, for which destination?

Confirm tenant and HUMAIN decision owners, self-review restrictions, whether tenant approval is a prerequisite, and who executes publication.

02 / Admission contract

What must every submission contain?

Agree package/manifest identity, permissions, test access, Eval evidence, intended use and minimum readiness checks. Publish these requirements inside the developer flow.

03 / Independent execution

What can the verifier actually run?

Confirm sandbox owner, isolation, data handling, supported agent types, Guardian input/output contract and responsibilities when a run is blocked.

04 / Decision policy

What blocks approval, and what requires retesting?

Name the common baseline, applicable additions, severity rules, evidence validity, change triggers and appeal path. Keep unresolved requirements visibly unresolved.

Recommended next step

Agree the review contract, then prototype a complete correction loop. Make it possible to follow one agent version from developer evidence to an accountable admission decision.

Evidence and limits

Public research checked on 24 September 2026. Recommendations on this page are a synthesis, not decisions attributed to vendors, Sandip or HUMAIN.

  1. Microsoft: review and publish an offer. Certification, corrective feedback and publisher sign-off.
  2. Google Cloud: publish your AI agent. Review requirements and the distinction between validation and public listing.
  3. Salesforce: AgentExchange security review. Marketplace review alongside the customer's own evaluation responsibility.
  4. AWS: update model approval status. Version-specific approval and configurable deployment behavior.
  5. NIST: AI RMF Core. Accountability and context; the source currently notes that a revision is in progress.
  6. OWASP: Agentic Applications Top 10, 2026. Agentic threat reference, not a complete test specification.
  7. SDAIA knowledge center and NCA Data Cybersecurity Controls. Reference families for policy clarification, not a determination of legal applicability.
