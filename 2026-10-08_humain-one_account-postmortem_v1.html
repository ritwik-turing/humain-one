HUMAIN ONE · Turing × HUMAIN KSA · Blameless account postmortem

5,806 engineering hours, no signed scope, no revenue

How Turing ran the HUMAIN account from the 26 March announcement to 8 October 2026, and why a strong team doing real work never produced a paid outcome.

Period 26 Mar to 8 Oct 2026 Sprints S0 to S10 Prepared 8 Oct 2026, Product Status Internal draft
Blameless by design

This review examines gates, ownership and operating choices. It does not assign fault to any person on either side. Everyone involved acted reasonably on the information and authority they had. The client relationship is healthy and the engineering was good. The question here is narrow: why did that work never turn into revenue?

01

The short version

Biggest reasons we failed
  1. We built before anything was signed. 5,806 hours went in against an SOW that stayed a draft from 28 Mar. No price, no revenue share and no named payer.
  2. The objective moved with the decision maker. 10 changes of objective and 4 hand-overs. 9 of the 10 changes landed within a sprint of a hand-over.
  3. Nobody owned turning the account into revenue. It was never classified as a lighthouse, revenue or capability account, and no path to revenue was written down.
  4. We discovered requests, not the problem. By 29 May there were still no success metrics and no agreed end state.
  5. We said yes and kept polishing. Half the objective changes were our own calls, and about 21% of hours became rework.
Learnings
  • An announcement is not a contract. Speed without a signed anchor turned into rework, not revenue.
  • One owner works. Since 24 Aug a single objective owner has kept decisions stable, and the Evaluations hub shipped.
  • Effort should follow conviction. Decide what kind of account it is before staffing it.
  • Every decision needs a name and a date. Meetings and stakeholders are no substitute for one accountable person.
  • Cap each proof. Set an engineering and inference budget before building, not after.
What to do next
  1. Name the economic buyer and the objective owner in writing with the client.
  2. Price the Evaluations hub as a signed SOW amendment or a paid pilot.
  3. Get RC-1 and RC-2 accepted so work already shipped becomes billable.
  4. Date the three blocked decisions: verifier persona, tenant contract and code-generation ownership.
  5. Cap new build until the first two actions land.

    05

    Root cause: five whys

    Why is there no revenue?

    No milestone was ever accepted as invoiceable, and no rate or price was ever set.

    Why was nothing invoiceable?

    There was no signed scope defining acceptance, price or payer. The SOW stayed a draft from 28 March onward.

    Why did building go ahead without it?

    The public announcement made speed look like the commercial priority. Announcing first was a sound call. Building without an anchor left neither side a document to point to when the premise moved.

    Why did the commercial track stay open for six months?

    No one on either side was accountable for converting the account. The account was never classified as a lighthouse, a revenue account or a reusable capability, so effort was never tied to a path to revenue.

    Root cause

    Turing entered an ambiguous, transformation-shaped engagement with a playbook built for defined execution work: staff first, build fast, say yes. It had no qualification gate, no paid discovery and no single owner per stage.

    What went well

    Delivery held under churn

    • Sprint 5 closed with 436 issues, 361 done, and incomplete work was preserved rather than marked done.
    • Sandbox provisioning on GKE, Terraform, the agent registry, deploy records, the Submit Agent flow and Group 1 observability UX all shipped.
    • Prototypes reached Evaluations V110, Agent Lifecycle V70, Public Marketplace V16, Tenant Admin V4 and the LEAP V10 keynote.

    The fix has already been tested

    • Since 24 Aug there has been one objective owner, and the Evaluations hub shipped against a stable direction.
    • Prism facts were settled on 1 Sep: no pass bar, no metrics attached at the start, and cost taken from tokens only.
    • Product decisions PD-001 to PD-013 have been logged since 9 Sep, so they no longer live only in meetings.

    The relationship stayed healthy

    • The client kept engaging in weekly reviews through 14 Sep, with specific, actionable feedback.
    • Board hygiene held: deferred work was labelled rather than deleted, and the H2O-O spine went to backlog with its history intact.
