The one-line takeaway: the winning B2B agent marketplaces converge on the same skeleton — trust evidence as first-class listing sections, a quality gate on what may call itself an "agent", a full pricing menu with entitlements decoupled from checkout, and admin-mediated activation ending inside the buyer's workspace. The sovereignty angle (run in-customer-environment, bill through the store) is already codified by AWS and Microsoft and is HUMAIN ONE's natural differentiator: SAR billing through the storefront, execution in-Kingdom.

1c · Round 3 — listing-card & certification metrics NEW

Third verified pass (23 sources · 105 claims extracted · 24 confirmed, 1 refuted) targeting what belongs on the listing card for an org buyer and what a credible certification panel shows.

Card anatomy: B2B tiles are metric-sparse — ratings are typically the only number, and no major platform publishes install/deployment counts on cards (usage evidence lives at detail level). What moves org buyers instead: free trials (#1 influence, 77%), transparent pricing (72% more likely to buy; hidden pricing is the #1 deterrent), time-to-value (57% expect ROI within 3 months), and security posture (81% weigh breach history; ~3 in 4 firms gate AI software harder than non-AI).
Certification: the reference model is Microsoft's two tiers — a yearly independently audited Certification (pen test + data-handling/privacy/security against SOC 2 / PCI DSS / ISO 27001-derived controls) vs a self-declared Publisher Attestation (80+ factors, published verbatim, explicitly unverified). Salesforce publishes the security-review pass date on the listing. No incumbent publishes AI-quality numbers (accuracy, hallucination rates, prompt-injection results) — publishing measured ones from our own evaluation harness, labelled with method and version, is a genuine differentiator; inventing them without a harness is disqualifying.

Applied to the V1 mock: cards now show cert-tier badge · Trust score · go-live time · trial line · transparent SAR price · residency tag (no usage counts); the listing's Certification tab splits MEASURED (harness, versioned) from PUBLISHER-ATTESTED (declared, unverified). Coverage gap: GPT Store / Poe / Smithery / AgentExchange card anatomy and Google/AWS AI-certification gates did not survive verification this round.

2 · The 10 design patterns that repeat across winners

  1. Trust sections are named listing anatomy, not marketing copy. Security & Compliance and Certification get their own tabs next to capabilities and usage. HIGH
  2. Mandatory pre-listing security review + certification badging. Salesforce attacks submissions; Microsoft demands security documentation; Google runs review + operations validation. HIGH
  3. Machine-readable agent cards and protocol declarations (MCP / A2A). Google requires an A2A Agent Card; AWS listings declare protocol support. HIGH
  4. A quality gate on the word "agent". AWS makes sellers attest to reasoning LLMs + autonomy or list as a tool; Microsoft requires differentiated value beyond native Copilot. HIGH
  5. The full pricing menu at launch: free · flat subscription (prorated) · usage-metered · combined · private offers for negotiated enterprise deals. HIGH
  6. An entitlement service decoupling checkout from activation. Purchases become quantified, checkable capacity units (seats, usage) the product must honor. HIGH
  7. Instant automated provisioning on offer acceptance. No fulfillment lag between paying and having. HIGH
  8. Admin scope assignment with a self-serve lane. Assign to me / org / groups, Block/Remove — but users can still self-install inside admin guardrails. Do not design IT-only. HIGH
  9. An in-customer-environment deployment tier for sovereignty buyers, billed through the marketplace. HIGH
  10. In-workspace, intent-based discovery — semantic search where the buyer already works ("automate invoice follow-up in Arabic"), not only a marketing storefront. HIGH

3 · The 5 failure modes to avoid

  1. Self-attested quality bars without audit. Attestation-only gates invite category pollution. MEDIUM
  2. Vetting that cannot scale with listing growth. Critics already question whether Salesforce's review keeps pace with 104% listing growth. GPT Store's spam problem (TechCrunch, Mar 2024) is the cautionary tale — quality collapse from light-touch moderation. MEDIUM · criticism evidence directional
  3. Fulfillment methods frozen at publication. AWS locks Quick-Launch-vs-redirect at publish; build ours to be changeable. HIGH
  4. Conflating store visibility with actual activation. Deployed ≠ used; instrument first-use, not installs. MEDIUM
  5. Hosting-only models that ignore data-residency buyers. SOC 2 / ISO 27001 badges alone do not cover agent-behavioral risk (prompt-injection misuse) — infrastructure certs are necessary, not sufficient. MEDIUM
  6. Cancellation that does not deprovision. NEW The sharpest documented buyer pain found: canceling an AWS Marketplace subscription does not stop charges until instances are separately terminated in every region, across two consoles. HUMAIN ONE rule: cancel = full deprovision, atomically — one action ends billing and tears down the runtime, mirroring our lifecycle mock's retire flow. HIGH

4 · Recommendations for the HUMAIN ONE Public Marketplace

Storefront & discovery

  • One public storefront (separate URL, per Andrew alignment) with an agent-vs-tool taxonomy and a stated quality bar for the "agent" label.
  • Category browse + semantic use-case search; curated collections ("For ministries", "Citizen services", "Back office").
  • Each listing backed by a machine-readable agent card (A2A-aligned) — this also future-proofs interop.

Listing page

  • Named sections mirroring the Microsoft pattern and our approved V9 lifecycle surface: Overview · Capabilities & data access · Security & Compliance · Certification (gov-citizen-v1 profile, in-Kingdom residency) · Performance & standing (Trust Score, indicative labelling) · Reviews & usage.
  • Trust evidence rendered exactly like the developer side — same Trust Score, same evidence chips — so the seller-side story and buyer-side story are one system.

Commerce (SAR)

  • Ship the standard menu: free tier · flat monthly SAR subscription (prorated) · SAR-metered usage · combined · private offers.
  • Mock checkout shows SAR pricing with 15% VAT line and ZATCA e-invoice issuance (flag: exact ZATCA/PDPL requirements are open homework — no source covered SAR tax handling; treat as inference to validate).
  • Entitlement service between checkout and activation: purchase → entitlement record (seats / invocations) → workspace checks entitlements before agent runs. Purchases can draw down platform credits (the MACC pattern) later.

Ratings & reviews design NEW

  • Launch with: verified-publisher badge · install/active-tenant count · curated Featured collections. No open star reviews at launch (Smithery and Gemini Enterprise both omit them; GPT Store shows what light-touch moderation does at volume).
  • If reviews ship later, gate them to verified purchasers only.

Sovereignty tier (corrected in round 2)

  • Offer two runtime tiers on the listing: HUMAIN-hosted (fastest) and in-Kingdom dedicated / in-tenant (the AWS-container / Azure-Application pattern) — billed through the storefront either way.
  • Correction R2: present in-Kingdom residency as a hosting-tier feature, not a blanket legal mandate — the 2024 CCC revision actually relaxed in-CCC localization requirements and shifted residency oversight toward NDMO/SDAIA, and PDPL transfer-rule enforcement claims did not survive verification.
  • Trust badge wording: cite NCA CCC-2:2024 / ECC assessment (the scheme behind CST Class C cloud licensing — the pattern Google, Oracle, and Huawei use for their Saudi regions). Do not cite the superseded CCC-1:2020.

KSA checkout compliance checklist NEW

  • 15% VAT as an explicit line item on the order summary; capture the buyer's VAT registration number.
  • B2B tax invoices follow ZATCA Phase 2 Clearance: invoice generated as XML, submitted to the FATOORA platform pre-issuance; ZATCA (not the seller) applies the cryptographic stamp and QR code; the cleared invoice is delivered to the buyer as XML or PDF/A-3 with embedded XML. A plain emailed PDF is non-compliant. (Wave 24 enforcement deadline: 30 Jun 2026.)
  • For the mock: after payment, show "ZATCA-cleared tax invoice" with a QR and an e-invoice reference chip — that is the defensible detail Andrew's side will recognize.

Trials NEW

  • Proven B2B mechanic: AWS-style seller-configurable free trial (7–90 days, one per account). Recommended HUMAIN ONE default: 14–30 day per-tenant trial, one per tenant, automatic hard stop — no silent conversion to paid — converting into a SAR contract offer.
  • Plus an always-available, no-signup interactive sandbox on the listing for lightweight agents (the GPT Store / Poe try-immediately pattern) — the conversion lever enterprise stores lack.

Activation & first use

  • Instant provisioning at payment acceptance (Salesforce pattern): "acquired" state within seconds.
  • Dual-path activation: org admin assigns (me / org / groups, block/remove) and individual users can self-acquire within guardrails.
  • The journey must end at first successful use in the buyer's workspace — a chat/session with the agent plus a first-run checklist (connect data, set scope, invite team). Instrument first-use as the success metric, not acquisition.

6 · Coverage gaps & open questions

What round 2 fixed: Gemini Enterprise, Smithery, and Poe are now covered (ratings, monetization, gating); the KSA checkout checklist (ZATCA Phase 2, VAT, CCC-2:2024, CST Class C) is verified against ZATCA and NCA primary documents; the trial mechanic is verified against AWS docs; and one concrete buyer-pain (cancellation ≠ deprovision) is documented from AWS's own knowledge base.
