The whole picture, in plain English

For you, before the meeting. Not a script.
The settingHUMAIN ONE is a marketplace. Outside developers build AI agents, small software workers that talk to citizens and do tasks, like answering questions about permits. A developer submits their agent, a human verifier decides whether it can be listed, and approved agents go live for a government body such as a municipality. This eval product is the workbench the developer uses to find out what is wrong with their agent and fix it, both before launch and after.
What an evaluation actually isYou cannot read an agent and know if it is good, the way you cannot read a driver and know if they can drive. The only way is to test it: ask it hundreds of realistic questions and judge what comes back. An evaluation is exactly that, a big list of test questions called cases, plus machinery that scores the answers. A run is one sitting of the whole test.
What "in production" meansBefore launch, the agent only ever answers test questions. Mistakes are free, nobody real is on the other end. "In production" means the agent is live: real citizens are talking to it, and a mistake now has consequences, wrong information given, or worse, one citizen shown another citizen's details. The same agent behaves differently against real people than against a test sheet, which is why evaluation cannot stop at launch.
Why the data refreshes dailyA test set goes stale. Real people ask things nobody predicted: a new policy arrives, a season changes, people phrase things in new ways. An agent that is only tested on last month's questions will pass the test and fail on the street. So the dataset syncs nightly from the data warehouse, and the product samples one in fifty real conversations, with personal details masked, into the test set. Yesterday's real traffic becomes today's exam. And when something fails in production, that exact conversation becomes a permanent test case, so the same failure can never slip through silently again. That is the closed loop the demo keeps referring to.
Why connect a warehouse instead of uploading filesOrganisations keep their records and logs in one central place, a data warehouse like Snowflake. Connecting to it means the test set updates itself from the source of truth. Uploading CSVs by hand means someone has to remember to do it, and the test set quietly drifts away from reality.
Who is whoThe developer builds the agent, reads its failures, writes notes and checks. The platform runs everything that needs no knowledge of the agent: did it finish, did it loop, did it leak data. The judge is an AI that grades answers at scale, cheap but only trustworthy once its grades are compared against a human's, which is what the agreement score is. The verifier is the human who decides marketplace entry. The tenant is the government body whose citizens use the agent.
Why there is no pass markA single score invites gaming: build to the test, hide the gaps. Instead the verifier receives everything, including a list of what was not checked, and makes a human decision. The gate is transparency, not a number.
Plain wordsCase: one test question with the answer you expect. Dataset: all the cases. Run: one pass of the agent over the dataset. Check: a yes-or-no question asked of every answer. Metric: a score across the whole run. Judge: an AI that grades answers. Attention set: the handful of cases picked out as worth a human look. Trace: the step-by-step record of what the agent did inside one case. Regression: something that used to work and now does not. Sampling: copying a small share of real traffic into the test set. PII masking: stripping names, numbers and identifiers before anyone sees a conversation.

Set up a run

Screen 1 · the three-layer metric board
WhatThe gov-services-eval dataset, 380 cases across 38 citizen journeys in Arabic and English, synced nightly from Snowflake, with connectors for CSV, warehouse, S3 or GCS, and sampled production traffic. Every card is typed: Dataset, Built-in checks, Judge metrics, Run strategies. Cost and run count update live as you pick.
WhyA metric's place is decided by what it needs as input, not how important it sounds. Checks that read only the run or the reply generalise to any agent, so they are defaults. Checks that need ground truth or repeat runs are choices with visible cost. Same split Azure AI Foundry ships: risk-and-safety evaluators recommended for every application type.
Say thisNothing here needs configuring to get value on day one. The developer can run the moment they upload, and every extra depth is priced before they commit.
Push back"Why can safety not be turned off?" A check the developer can disable is not a guarantee the marketplace can make. The developer most likely to leak citizen data is the one least likely to tick the box.

Results

Screen 2 · where the two-case argument lives
WhatA nine-metric board covering the whole palette, each cell opening per-case evidence, sparklines across the full run history, the safety band, and one complete case table: all 380 rows, paginated, with Needs attention as its default filter. Attention is a view of the table, never a smaller table.
WhyTwo cases in the demo pass every automatic column and still raise a safety flag: a cross-record phone number leak and a prompt injection from an uploaded document. Without always-on answer checks both are invisible. Step repetition (15.7%) plus mishandled termination (12.4%) is about a quarter of observed failures in MAST, Cemri et al. 2025, arXiv 2503.13657. The rest needs judgement.
Say thisEvery metric in your palette is on this one screen, and every number opens the cases behind it. Then watch case 8 and case 11: green across every column, and both are the kind of failure that ends up in a newspaper. That is why safety is not a toggle.
Push back"How do you know the judge is right?" We do not yet, and the card says so on screen: judge scores are labelled signal, not measurement, until judge-vs-human agreement ships. The design follows the published calibration loop, sample, hand-grade, compute agreement, recalibrate on change, and well-calibrated judges now agree with humans about as often as two humans agree with each other. Stating the limit beats having it extracted from us.

What the agent did / What went wrong

Screens 3 and 4 · the developer improvement loop
WhatStep-by-step trace with timing bars and payloads, a notes box in the developer's own words, and notes auto-grouped and counted with rename and merge.
WhyEvaluation criteria cannot be fixed before seeing outputs; practitioners refine them by reading failures. The product operationalises exactly that loop. Criteria drift: Shankar et al., UIST 2024. Error analysis via open coding: standard practice in applied LLM evaluation.
Say thisWe never see the developer's code and never suggest a fix. We make the failure legible and count what they tell us, which is what actually gets agents fixed.
Push back"Is a free-text note rigorous enough?" The notes become groups, groups become counts, and counts prioritise the next fix. It is the documented workflow of the strongest eval teams, not a compromise.

Turn notes into checks

Screen 5 · notes become permanent tests, and the judge earns trust
WhatA note the developer wrote becomes a saved check that runs on every case of every future run. Before anyone relies on a check, the product shows how often it agreed with the developer on cases they graded by hand: 17 of 20 here, with the three disagreements listed row by row.
WhyEvaluation criteria drift as developers read outputs, so automated judges must be validated against human grades rather than assumed correct. Showing the agreement record before a check gates anything is the published state of the art. EvalGen, Shankar et al., UIST 2024: "Who Validates the Validators?" The 2026 industry standard formalises the same loop: hand-grade a sample, compute judge-to-human agreement, accept at kappa 0.6 and above, recalibrate monthly.
Say thisMost tools let you write a judge and trust it blindly. Here the judge earns trust: it shows you exactly where it disagrees with you before it ever scores a run, and every saved check carries that record into the evidence pack.
Push back"Is 20 hand-graded cases enough?" It is the starting signal, and the screen says exactly how many grades back the number. Every case the developer reviews afterwards sharpens it. The alternative shipped by everyone else is zero validation.

What broke since last time

Screen 6 · regressions, including invisible ones
WhatTwo most recent runs compared per case, with a band for new safety flags even when no column changed.
WhyThe scariest regression is the one the columns cannot show: the demo contains a fix that introduces a prompt-injection flag while every mechanical value stays identical.
Say thisThe developer's own fix caused this flag. The screen separates "your change broke this" from "this was already broken", which is the difference between panic and a plan.
Push back"Only two runs?" The across-runs strip on this screen and the sparkline card on results cover the full history: pass rate 74 to 97 percent over five runs, accuracy up 14 points, and the safety line dips then rises when the bad fix lands. Detail stays two-run because that is the decision a developer actually makes: keep this change or revert it.

Evidence and Submit

Screens 7 and 8 · what the verifier receives
WhatAuto-assembled pack: every check listed with what happened to it, including the ones that did not run and why. Open issues declared, not hidden. Submit is never gated by a score.
WhySilence about a metric reads the same as a pass, which is how certifications get torn apart. Stating coverage explicitly is what separates an assessed tier from a self-declared one. Mirrors tiered attestation standards for agent vendors; goes beyond Microsoft Marketplace, which runs no output evaluation at all.
Say thisThere is no pass bar. Evaluation earns its place by being useful, and the verifier sees exactly what was and was not checked, so trust comes from coverage rather than a single number.
Push back"No minimum score, really?" The gate is the verifier plus total transparency of coverage. A bar invites gaming the number; a coverage statement invites fixing the agent.

The end state, end to end

Where this product goes, one loop
IngestDatasets arrive from a warehouse table (Snowflake, BigQuery, Databricks), a bucket of logs (S3, GCS), a CSV, or sampled production traffic after PII masking. Mapping happens once per source.
EvaluateAlways-on mechanical and safety checks on every case, judge metrics where the category has a reviewed answer set, run strategies (bias, robustness, reliability) priced before you commit.
ImproveFailures cluster into an attention set. The developer reads traces, writes notes, notes become groups, groups become saved checks, and every check carries its agreement record before it scores anything.
CertifyRun history shows the arc, the regression view catches what a fix broke including invisible safety regressions, and the evidence pack states what was and was not checked for the verifier. No pass bar; the gate is transparency.
MonitorPublished agents feed sampled production conversations back into the dataset. A production failure becomes tomorrow morning attention case, which closes the loop: the same screens serve pre-release evaluation and live monitoring.
Say thisEvaluation is not a stage the agent passes through once. It is the loop the marketplace runs forever, and every part of that loop is on these screens today.

Objection bank

Cross-cutting
Is this state of the art?The ACL 2026 survey of agent evaluation names cost-efficiency, safety and robustness as the critical gaps in the field. Those are exactly the three this product treats as first class: cost on every estimate before you run, safety always on, robustness one toggle away with its price shown.
Cost figuresIllustrative at 0.048 SAR per case, consistent everywhere. Real unit economics slot in without changing the design.
Arabic187 of the 380 cases are Arabic, rendered right to left in the dataset browser, and the answer-language check is always on. Open the browser and click the Arabic filter to show it live.
Why focus on 12 of 380?Nobody reviews 380 rows one by one, so the table defaults to the 12 representative cases clustered from the failures, the same triage pattern issue trackers use. Click All cases and the same table pages through every one of the 380. Nothing is hidden; attention is a filter, not a subset product.
Any agent?Layer 1 needs nothing from the developer, so it works for any agent on first upload. Only category scores need a curated answer set, and the product says so instead of hiding it.
