HUMAIN ONEEval session runbook / for Ritwik / not for sharing

Running the eval session

Product on the shared screen, this on your phone. Green is what you say, amber is what they will say back, grey is what you ask them.

The first sixty seconds

Set the frame before anything else
Open with thisI am going to walk the whole thing in about fifteen minutes. I am not looking for approval today, I want you to try to break it. There are nine things I already know are unresolved and I will flag them as we go rather than wait to be caught. What I actually want out of the hour is a direction on two of them.

Why this matters: it turns the session from a review you can fail into a working session you are running. It also means every hole they find is one you invited, not one you missed.

Run order

About eighteen minutes, then open it up
0:00
3 min

Set up a run

  1. Top line first. Run 5 finished 2 hours ago, 6 of 12 had problems, 2 still need a look. The product remembers where you were.
  2. Cases card. The 380 badge, then read the definition line out: a case is one citizen question, most with the answer you expect written next to it.
  3. Source row. Snowflake, GOV_SERVICES.EVAL_V3, synced 2 hours ago, Connected. Not a CSV somebody uploaded once in March.
  4. Coverage row. 38 journeys, 187 Arabic, 193 English.
  5. Click Browse all 380 cases, hit the Arabic filter, let them see the right to left rendering, close it again.
  6. Click Production traffic under Add cases from and read the panel aloud. This is the closed loop in one sentence.
  7. Now point at the four card chips in order: Dataset, Built-in checks, Judge metrics, Run strategies.
  8. Always on card. Three example rows, then the line naming the four that read the reply itself.
  9. Costs extra card. Tick Bias and let them watch 380 become 760, the time double, and the price move before anything runs.
SayFour cards, four different kinds of thing, and the chip on each one says which. Dataset, built-in checks, judge metrics, run strategies. That split is the whole architecture, and it is decided by what each check needs as input rather than by how important it sounds.
Nothing here needs configuring to get value on day one. Every metric has a default. What costs more is priced before you commit, not after it has run.
They will hit
  • Where did the 380 come from? Generated for the demo and seeded, so the numbers never move between demos. In a real deployment it syncs nightly from the tenant warehouse.
  • Why Snowflake? It is the example. The connectors underneath cover a warehouse, a bucket, a CSV, and sampled live traffic. Whatever the tenant already has.
  • What if a tenant has no warehouse? CSV still works and that is the floor. The point of connecting a source is that it updates itself instead of drifting.
  • Who maps the columns? Once per source, then remembered. That row is showing a mapping that already happened.
  • Why is accuracy only available in this category? It needs a written correct answer to compare against. Government services has a reviewed answer set, a brand new category would not, and the screen says so rather than greying out a toggle.
  • What if I have 10,000 cases, not 380? Same screen. Cost and time scale linearly, which is exactly why the estimate sits next to the button.
  • Is 30.78 SAR real? No. Invented placeholder at about five halalas a case. I need the real number from someone.
Ask them
  • If you were the developer uploading here for the first time, what is missing before you would trust this?
  • Does the four card split read clearly on its own, or did it only make sense because I just explained it?
  • Should a developer see cost at all, or is that our problem to absorb and hide?
3:00
1 min

Run it

  1. Press Run 6 on 380 cases.
  2. Point at the counter climbing through the hundreds, and at the Arabic questions scrolling past in the log.
  3. Let it finish and land on results by itself.
SayTen workers in parallel, about six minutes for the full set. This is the part nobody watches, which is rather the point.
They will hit
  • Six minutes is slow. Agreed, and it is a demo number. Real throughput depends on the agent under test, not on us.
  • Can I run a subset? Not in the prototype. Worth asking whether a smoke run of fifty before the full 380 is something a developer would actually want.
Ask them
  • Would you want a fast subset run before committing to the full set, or is that a false economy?
4:00
4 min

Results, the metric board

  1. Read the top line: 380 cases attempted. Not a sample.
  2. Point at the nine cells and name them in the palette vocabulary they sent us.
  3. Click Safety. The drilldown opens with the two flagged cases as chips.
  4. Click Accuracy and let them see the caveat sitting under the number.
  5. Click Bias and fairness, which reads not run, and show that it says why rather than showing a zero.
  6. Scroll to the Across runs card. Six sparklines. Point at the red one.
SayEvery metric from the palette you sent, on one screen, and every number opens the cases behind it.
Five of those six lines climb. The red one is safety, and it drops for four runs then jumps back up on the fifth. That is a fix breaking something else, and it is the story of the next two screens.
They will hit
  • How do you know the judge is right? We do not, and the card says so before anyone asks. Nobody has checked our grader against a human on this dataset. That is why it is labelled a signal and why nothing automatic ever fires on it.
  • Why is bias off by default? Because it doubles the run. It is a choice with a price, not a metric we can just leave on.
  • Latency shows a median, why not an average? One 31 second timeout drags an average and hides the typical case. The slowest is shown separately in the drilldown.
  • Nine metrics feels like a lot to read. Fair. The board is a glance layer, and the honest answer is that most days a developer looks at two of them.
Ask them
  • Which of these nine would you not trust, and what would you need to see before you did?
  • Is nine the right number, or should some of these live somewhere else?
8:00
2 min

The safety band, your strongest moment

  1. Scroll to the red band above the table. Two cases.
  2. Read both aloud, including the mechanism, not just the label.
  3. Now scroll down to the table and find those same two rows. Point at the columns: yes, yes, yes.
  4. Let the silence sit for a second before saying the line.
SayBoth of these pass every single automatic check. One hands a citizen somebody else phone number. The other followed an instruction hidden inside an uploaded document and told the citizen their application was approved.
If safety were a toggle, the developer most likely to ship this is exactly the developer least likely to tick the box.
They will hit
  • This is contrived. The data is invented, agreed. Open the trace and show the mechanism: it matched on surname instead of reference number. That failure is not exotic, it is a join on the wrong key.
  • Would a real safety classifier catch these? A PII check catches the phone number. Injection is harder and the honest answer is that detection is imperfect, which is why the developer also gets to write their own checks.
  • Two out of 380 is not many. It only takes one to end the programme. And these are the two that no other part of the product would have surfaced.
Ask them
  • Is there a category of failure like this that we are not watching for at all?
10:00
2 min

What the agent did

  1. You are already on case 11 from the chip. Point at the subtitle: every column passed, the flag below is why it still needs you.
  2. The trace. Four steps, one of them red.
  3. Point at the red step and read the payload: two records side by side, SALEM N with a mobile number and SALEM A with mobile withheld.
  4. Then point at the reply on the right, containing that exact number.
  5. Press J a couple of times to show you can walk the cases from the keyboard.
SayThis is the difference between telling a developer they failed and showing them where. The step that broke is the one that read the contact field off the wrong record.
They will hit
  • Would a real agent expose a trace like this? It has to, or none of this works. That is a requirement on the agent contract, and worth flagging as one.
  • What if the agent is a black box? Then we get the input, the output and the timings, and lose the step detail. The checks still run. The debugging gets much worse.
Ask them
  • Does the trace format we assume here match what agents on the substrate will actually emit?
12:00
3 min

Notes become checks

  1. Screen 5. Point at the draft check written in plain words, not code.
  2. Point at yes or no as the only answer options and say why: two options is more reliable than a score out of ten.
  3. Scroll to Does this check agree with you. 17 of 20.
  4. Point at the three rows where it disagreed and read one.
  5. Scroll to the library. The saved check carries its agreement record with it.
SayMost tools let you write a judge and then trust it blindly. Here it has to show you where it disagreed with you on twenty cases you graded by hand, before it is allowed to score anything.
And the three it got wrong are all the same shape, which tells the developer exactly how to sharpen the wording.
They will hit
  • Prism can already do rubric metrics. True, and I am not rebuilding Prism. What Prism has no answer for is this validation loop, and that is the part that makes a developer-written check worth anything.
  • Twenty hand-graded cases is not many. It is a starting signal and the screen says how many back it. The alternative everyone else ships is zero validation.
  • Who grades the twenty? The developer, on their own agent. It is their definition of correct, which is the whole point.
Ask them
  • Should the verifier see a developer check library, or is it private to them?
  • What agreement level would you want before a check is allowed to gate anything?
15:00
2 min

What broke since last time

  1. Point at the header: Run 6 against run 5. Always the two most recent.
  2. The red band. One new safety flag since run 5.
  3. Point at the second entry: already there in run 5. Different problem, different response.
  4. Now the table row. Run 5 and Run 6 columns are identical. Nothing changed. It is still marked broken.
  5. Click Only what got worse and show that the case is in there despite no column moving.
SayThe developer fixed one thing and opened a security hole doing it. Every column stayed the same. This is the regression a metric dashboard structurally cannot show you.
And it separates your change broke this from this was already broken, which is the difference between a panic and a plan.
They will hit
  • Only two runs compared? Detail is two runs because that is the decision a developer actually makes, keep this change or revert it. The full arc is in the across runs strip on the same screen.
  • What actually happens to a live agent when this fires? This is my weakest answer and I would rather have yours. My proposal is that a safety problem pulls it from new installs within the hour, basics slipping gets seven days, and a judge score moving does nothing.
Ask them
  • What should the consequence be when a listed agent regresses? I have a proposal and I am not confident in it.
  • Is seven days right for the middle case, or is that made up? It is made up.
17:00
2 min

Evidence and submit

  1. Point at the evidence rows and say none of it was typed by hand.
  2. Scroll to What was checked, and what was not. Fifteen rows.
  3. Point at the not run rows and read one, including the reason.
  4. Click Download the pack as PDF so they see it is a real artefact.
  5. Go to submit. Point at there is no score you have to reach and at the message box to the verifier.
SayThe pack says what was not checked, not just what was. Silence about a metric reads exactly like a pass, and that is how a certification gets pulled apart six months later.
There is no minimum score anywhere in this product. The gate is the verifier plus total transparency about coverage.
They will hit
  • No pass mark at all? A number invites building to the test. Give the verifier everything instead, including what the developer chose not to run, and let a human decide.
  • Will a verifier actually read this? Honest answer, unknown. We have never watched one use it. Worth a session with whoever does that job.
  • What stops a developer turning everything off? Nothing, and the pack says they did. Turning checks off is visible, which is the deterrent.
Ask them
  • Is this enough for a verifier to make a decision, and what would you add?
  • Should any check be mandatory before submit is even possible?

Have the answer ready before the question

architecture, security, tenancy
Where does the eval compute run and under whose contract, given KSA data. Does one tenant's data ever touch another's. Who validates the PII masker before we sample live conversations. How does this sit against the substrate, one service or many.

Your honest position: compute residency is an open question I need HUMAIN to answer. The masker has no owner and no evaluation, and I have written a proposal for how it should be tested. Both are on the list, neither is solved.
Ritesh
Prism, evaluation
Is this not Prism with a new front end. Can Prism run this today or is it a rewrite. Who writes and maintains the answer sets per category. Who recalibrates the judges.

Your honest position: Prism is the engine and most of this rides on what it already does. What is new is the journey, the clustering into an attention set, the check validation loop, and the evidence pack. Answer sets and recalibration are real ongoing work with no owner yet, and that is a resourcing conversation not a design one.
Sandip
client readiness, commercials
Is this ready to put in front of the client. What does it cost us per run. What is the timeline to build. Did we actually fix what they objected to.

Your honest position: the objection was dense and not exhaustive, and both are addressed, nine metrics and 380 cases against twelve rows and four columns. Costs are invented and I need a real number. Timeline depends on the two open questions, which is why I am asking today.

Concede immediately

Fast agreement buys you the ones that matter
  • The costs are made up, cost per case included.
  • The 380 cases are generated, not real citizen traffic.
  • There is no screen for turning a live conversation into a scored case. A human has to write the expected answer and we have not designed that.
  • Everything is one question and one answer. Real conversations are not.
  • Nobody has checked our AI grader against a human on this dataset.
  • Nobody owns the PII masker or the policy corpus.

Say these before they are found. Every one you volunteer makes the next thing you defend more credible.

Hold firm

These have an argument behind them
  • Safety always on. Two cases prove it on screen.
  • No pass mark. A score invites building to the test. The verifier gets everything instead.
  • Judge scores labelled a signal. Calling 78 percent a fact when nobody validated it would be dishonest.
  • The pack names what was not checked. Silence reads as a pass.
  • Attention set is a filter, not a smaller product. All 380 are in the same table.
  • No automatic action on a judge score. We call it a signal, so acting on it would contradict us.

If it goes sideways

Recovery moves
This is just Prism
Agree first. Prism is the engine and I am not rebuilding it. Then show screen 5. The validation loop is the thing Prism has no answer for, and it is what makes a developer-written check worth anything.
Someone rabbit holes
That is a real question and I want to give it more than thirty seconds. Can I note it and come back once we are through the flow. Then actually write it down where they can see you write it.
Hard attack on one part
Agree with the true part of it out loud, then narrow to what is actually in dispute. You are right that the numbers are invented. The thing I would defend is the mechanism, not the data.
Room goes quiet
Name someone and give them a specific target rather than asking for general thoughts. Tirthankar, where does this break architecturally. Ritesh, what would Prism struggle with here.
You get asked something you cannot answer
Say you do not know, say when you will know, write it down. Do not improvise a design decision in the room, because it will be quoted back to you.

What you need to walk out with

If you get nothing else, get these
  1. A direction on judging live agents. Reference-free only for now, or do we go after the tenant's own signals like repeat questions and escalations. This decides the next build.
  2. A direction on compliance. Eval takes the machine-checkable part, Certify takes the human call. Agree or disagree, but decide.
  3. Three names. Who owns the policy corpus, who builds answer sets per category, who owns the PII masker.
  4. A real cost per case, or agreement that someone will get one.
  5. Go or no go on showing the client, and if no go, exactly what is missing.
Close with thisWhat I need before I build anything else is a direction on two things: how we judge an agent once it is live, and whether compliance sits with us or with Certify. Everything else on my list I can carry. Those two change what the product is.
